## What's Happening

I'm doing Thanksgiving a little differently this year—splitting it across two days and trying out koji on the turkey.

**Thursday (Thanksgiving Day)**: Bake dinner rolls and a cheesecake for tomorrow. Make turkey stock from the carcass (which also got some koji for extra umami). Have a light Japanese-American dinner while the stock simmers—turkey broth soba, edamame, salad with crispy turkey skin.

**Friday (Turkey Day)**: The actual feast. Turkey that's been marinating in shio-koji for 36 hours, then air-dried for crispy skin. All the usual sides—Brussels sprouts with sausage, hasselback potatoes, stuffing, gravy. Plus the rolls and desserts from yesterday.

---

## Why Koji?

Shio-koji is fermented rice with salt. The enzymes break down proteins, which makes meat more tender and adds a lot of umami flavor. It's basically a marinade that does more work than regular salt water. Also I think it sounds interesting.

The process:
- Made shio-koji 6 days ahead (kept it in a sous-vide bath at 91°F to speed it up)
- Butchered turkey into parts, coated in koji, vacuum sealed for 36 hours
- Wiped it off, let the skin air-dry in the fridge for 36 hours
- Roast on Friday

## The Timeline

- **6 days before**: Start shio-koji fermentation (sous-vide at 91°F)
- **4 days before**: Turkey starts thawing in fridge
- **2 days before**: Butcher turkey, apply koji marinade, vacuum seal
- **1 day before**: Remove from koji, start air-drying skin
- **Thursday**: Bake rolls and cheesecake, make stock, have fusion dinner
- **Friday 12pm**: Turkey in oven
- **Friday 2pm**: Eat

Everything's planned backwards from eating at 2pm on Friday.

---

## Notes

The two-day thing makes it less chaotic. Stock is done and cold when I need it for gravy. Bread is already baked. Dessert is made. Turkey just needs to roast. 

The Japanese-fusion Thursday dinner uses the stock while it's cooking, so nothing goes to waste and it bridges the two days together.

The gravy uses a mix of thickeners (roux, potato starch, gelatin, xanthan) because I wanted it to have body without being heavy or pasty.
